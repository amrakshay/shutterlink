# Security

## The threat model, stated plainly

ShutterLink makes the phone it is installed on act as a Bluetooth keyboard, and
sends one keystroke to a phone you have paired it with. That is the entire
surface. It opens no camera, reads no photos, touches no network and stores
nothing.

**What this app protects against:**

- **A stranger pairing with it unnoticed.** Pairing is the platform's own, not
  ours: the phone is only discoverable when you tap "Make this phone visible",
  for five minutes, and completing a bond needs the matching six-digit code
  accepted on both phones. Nothing here can be made to pair silently.
- **Your photos leaving the room.** The app never sees them. Photos are taken by
  your own camera app and stay on that phone; there is no transfer, no server,
  no account, no analytics, and no `INTERNET` permission in the APK — CI fails
  the build if one appears.

**What it does not protect against, and cannot:**

- **Someone with physical access to either phone.** If they can unlock it, they
  can use the app.
- **A paired phone staying paired.** A Bluetooth bond outlives the app. Once the
  two phones are bonded, anything this app sends is a keystroke that phone will
  accept while it is in range and the app is running. Unpair from the other
  phone's Bluetooth settings to end that, exactly as you would with a lost
  selfie remote.
- **What a keystroke does over there.** The app sends one key; the camera app in
  the foreground decides what that means. On a different app or a different
  screen, the same key does whatever that screen does with it. It is a keyboard,
  and a keyboard is only as safe as what is in front of it.

## Permissions, and why each exists

Four permissions, all Bluetooth.

| Permission | Why | When |
|---|---|---|
| `BLUETOOTH_ADVERTISE` | So your other phone can find this one to pair with. This app is the thing being found. | Android 12+ |
| `BLUETOOTH_CONNECT` | Sending the keystroke that fires the shutter. | Android 12+ |
| `BLUETOOTH`, `BLUETOOTH_ADMIN` | The same two on Android 11 and below, where the above do not exist. Capped with `maxSdkVersion="30"`. | Android ≤ 11 |

That is the whole list.

Earlier versions of this app also held `CAMERA`, `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION`,
`ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION`, `NEARBY_WIFI_DEVICES`, `ACCESS_WIFI_STATE`,
`CHANGE_WIFI_STATE`, `READ_MEDIA_IMAGES` and two foreground-service
permissions. Every one of them existed to stream a viewfinder between two
phones or to carry a photo back. None of that happens any more, so none of them
are requested — and `BLUETOOTH_SCAN` is gone too, because the app is found
rather than looking.

### What the built APK actually contains

The table above is what *we* declare. What ships is what matters, so CI checks the permissions in
the **built APK** with `aapt2`, not our manifest and not an intermediate — libraries add
permissions of their own, and a check that read only our file would be hollow.

One entry in the APK comes from somewhere other than our manifest:

| Permission | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| `DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION` | Generated by AndroidX, scoped to this app (`dev.amrakshay.shutterlink.…`). It exists so runtime-registered broadcast receivers are not exported to other apps — it restricts access rather than granting any. |

Google Play Services used to merge `ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE` and `DUMP` in through Nearby
Connections. That dependency is gone, and so are they.

**There is no `INTERNET` permission in the APK.** CI fails the build if one appears. That makes the
claim structural rather than a promise: without it, Android will not let the app open a network
socket whatever its code attempts. The two phones reach each other over classic Bluetooth, which
is not the internet, and the app works with no network of any kind available.

If a permission appears in the APK that is not in these tables, that is a bug — please report it.

## Reporting a vulnerability

Open an issue for anything non-sensitive. For something that should not be public first, contact
the maintainer directly rather than filing it; a fix and a release will follow before the details
are published.

Please include the two phone models and Android versions — most of the interesting behaviour here
is OEM-specific.
