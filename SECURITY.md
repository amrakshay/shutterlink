# Security

## The threat model, stated plainly

Two phones in one room talk directly to each other. One is showing a live camera feed; the other
can fire its shutter and pull photos off it. That is worth being careful about, and worth being
honest about what is and is not protected.

**What this app protects against:**

- **A stranger's phone joining your session uninterrupted.** Connections are not silent: both
  phones show a short confirmation code, and the person holding each one has to accept before
  anything is streamed. An attacker within Bluetooth range cannot connect while nobody is looking
  at the screens.
- **Your photos leaving the room.** There is no server, no account, no analytics and no internet
  permission path in the app's design. The far phone keeps the original; the near phone gets a
  copy over the direct link.

**What it does not protect against, and cannot:**

- **Someone with physical access to either phone.** If they can unlock it, they can use the app.
- **A determined attacker in radio range during an active session.** Nearby Connections encrypts
  the link, but the pairing is a short human-checked code, not a certificate. Treat the preview
  stream as confidential-ish, not secret.
- **The other camera app.** When you use "capture with another app" (see `docs/PLAN.md` §11),
  that app's own behaviour, permissions and network access are its business, not ours.

## Permissions, and why each exists

The app asks for a lot, and it should be able to justify every one.

| Permission | Why | When |
|---|---|---|
| `CAMERA` | It is a camera. | Phase 1 |
| `BLUETOOTH_SCAN`, `BLUETOOTH_ADVERTISE`, `BLUETOOTH_CONNECT` | Finding the other phone, being findable by it, and connecting. Android 12+. | Phase 2 |
| `BLUETOOTH`, `BLUETOOTH_ADMIN` | The same thing on Android 11 and below, where the three above do not exist. Capped with `maxSdkVersion="30"` so they are not requested on versions that have the finer-grained ones. | Phase 2, Android ≤ 11 |
| `ACCESS_WIFI_STATE`, `CHANGE_WIFI_STATE` | Nearby Connections needs them to form the Wi-Fi Direct group that carries the video. Neither reads your network traffic or the networks you have joined. | Phase 2 |
| `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION` | Android 10–12 refuse *any* BLE scan or Wi-Fi Direct discovery without it. The app does not read your location and does not want it; `maxSdkVersion="32"` means it is never even requested on Android 13 and up. | Phase 2, Android ≤ 12 only |
| `NEARBY_WIFI_DEVICES` | The Android 13+ replacement for the above, declared `neverForLocation` so the system itself enforces that we cannot derive a location from it. | Phase 2, Android 13+ |
| `FOREGROUND_SERVICE` | The far phone has to keep streaming with its screen off. The notification is required and deliberate — you should be able to see that it is running. | Phase 6 |
| `READ_MEDIA_IMAGES` | Picking up a photo another camera app just saved, so it can be sent back to you. | Phase 5.5 |
| `SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW` | Only for third-party camera apps that cannot hand a photo back directly; it is what lets ShutterLink return to the foreground afterwards. Requested at the moment you first use such an app, never on launch. | Phase 5.5, optional |

### What the built APK actually contains

The table above is what *we* declare. What ships is what matters, so CI checks the permissions in
the **built APK** with `aapt2`, not our manifest and not an intermediate — libraries add
permissions of their own, and a check that read only our file would be hollow.

Two entries in the APK come from somewhere other than our manifest:

| Permission | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| `ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE` | Merged by Google Play Services, which uses it to decide whether a transport is usable. Kept: it reveals whether you are on a network, not what you do on it, and removing it risks breaking the connection layer. |
| `DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION` | Generated by AndroidX, scoped to this app (`dev.amrakshay.shutterlink.…`). It exists so runtime-registered broadcast receivers are not exported to other apps — it restricts access rather than granting any. |

Play Services also merges `android.permission.DUMP` into the intermediate manifest, but it does
**not** survive into the APK, so the app never ships it.

**There is no `INTERNET` permission in the APK.** CI fails the build if one appears. That makes the
claim structural rather than a promise: without it, Android will not let the app open a network
socket whatever its code attempts. The two phones reach each other over Bluetooth and Wi-Fi Direct,
neither of which is the internet.

If a permission appears in the APK that is not in these tables, that is a bug — please report it.

## Reporting a vulnerability

Open an issue for anything non-sensitive. For something that should not be public first, contact
the maintainer directly rather than filing it; a fix and a release will follow before the details
are published.

Please include the two phone models and Android versions — most of the interesting behaviour here
is OEM-specific.
